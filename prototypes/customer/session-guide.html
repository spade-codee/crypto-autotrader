Customer experience study · 5–10 people · 30–40 minutes

Find the moments
that don’t make sense.

Let people try the prototype in their own words and at their own pace. Watch what they understand before you explain.

Go to the tasks ↓

Keep this guide on your device; show the participant only the prototype. Scenario links open a new tab with fresh sample state. Notes are handwritten; this page collects and saves nothing.

Before you begin

“This is a prototype. Every account figure is made up. Nothing is connected to any account. We’re testing the design, not you. Please think aloud.”

Use a participant code rather than a name. Note their trading experience and the UI commit used. Never ask for, look at, or accept keys, passwords, or screenshots of real accounts.

Don’t explain unless they are stuck. When you help, record where and what you said. Mark each task unaided, with help or not done, and capture their words and hesitation.

First, listen

Three background questions

  1. Do you hold any crypto now? Mostly BTC, mostly USDT, or something else — and what do you use it for?
  2. Have you ever sold, or held on, during a big drop and regretted it?
  3. Have you used a trading bot or copy-trading before? What happened?

Try the prototype

16 tasks. Seven starting points.

Read the question without the reference answer. Keep the same tab for tasks within a scenario so the participant’s progress stays in place.

Scenario 1

First visit and practice

Open scenario 1 in a new tab↗
Task 1

“In your own words, what does this do — and what doesn't it do?”

Facilitator reference

BTC only; one decision a day; moves the whole account; can lose money

Task 2

“Based on what you saw, what happens to most of its trades?”

Facilitator reference

Most completed cycles lost a little; a few long trends made up for them

Task 3

“Could it ever lose more than the 27.4% shown?”

Facilitator reference

Yes — the past worst drop is not a limit

Task 4

“If you went live, which of your money would it control?”

Facilitator reference

Every BTC and USDT in the dedicated account, including money added later

Task 5 · time this task

“Start practising.”

Facilitator reference

Done unaided; time it

Task 6

“What's the difference between connecting and activating?”

Facilitator reference

Connecting only checks and reads; activating starts trading, after confirming the funds, and can trade within about 15 minutes

Finish by listening

What would make this useful?

  1. What was the most confusing moment?
  2. Knowing that most completed cycles in the test lost money, would you still want this? Why?
  3. Would you practise first? For how long?
  4. If this cost a yearly fee in USDT, what would make it worth paying for?

Don’t quote a price. The pilot sets it.

After the first 2–3 sessions

Compare where people needed help or misunderstood the risk, funds controlled, fees or next action. Fix the worst confusions before the remaining sessions. Record which version each person saw.

At the end, summarise failed tasks, reasons, changes and what you learned about where the practice pilot belongs, the first invited group, whether annual growth should ever be shown, and when newly active accounts should first act (D1–D4). Keep observations separate from your interpretation.
